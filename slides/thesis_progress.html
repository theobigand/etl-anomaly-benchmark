The question

Does machine learning in an ETL pipeline beat plain rules, and for which anomalies?

Rules are the industry default: auditable, cheap, and blind to what the author did not anticipate. Recent work puts ML detectors inside the flow, but on one algorithm and one dataset. This thesis builds the missing head-to-head benchmark and breaks the result down by anomaly type.

What generic benchmarks leave out

Pipeline. Algorithms are scored outside any pipeline; integration cost is not measured.

Rules. The declarative baseline that dominates production is absent.

Types. One aggregate score per dataset hides which family finds which defect.

Threshold. One flag threshold, not the precision-recall trade-off.

The system

One interface, one measured path

extract
read the table
transform
exact dedup, clean
detect
fit + predict + score
load
write the files
the stage whose latency is measured, in real in-pipeline time

The demo and every benchmark call the same detect() stage. A continuous score() on top of the boolean predict() makes ROC-AUC and AP computable; detect(fit_df=...) fits on the train slice and scores the test set.

Exact deduplication belongs to the ordinary transform. Typed injected duplicates remain in their separate evaluation with transform_dedup.

    def predict(self, df) -> Series:  # bool/row

    def score(self, df) -> Series:    # graded

Metric interpretation

Average Precision measures the ranking

AP = Σ ΔR · P
precision, weighted by each recall increment, over distinct score cutoffs from high to low; tied scores enter together. AP uses continuous anomaly scores. Precision, recall, F1 and the flagged share use a selected threshold.
Non-interpolated

AP differs from trapezoidal precision-recall area.

Prevalence matters

Prevalence is the no-skill reference. Constant scores give AP equal to prevalence.

Decisions apart from scores

Changing only the flag threshold leaves AP and ROC-AUC unchanged; neither fixes a useful operating point.

Historical naming: stored pr_auc fields and remaining AP labels in archived figures and the paper denote this same AP quantity.

Fitted rules

Column bounds do not fix row alert rates

Per-column tails under a Gaussian: 0.27% beyond 3σ, 0.70% beyond the IQR fences (about 2.7σ). Fitted bounds and several columns change row-level rates.

Amber: the region a rule flags. Gaussian reference only.

Z-score

Max |x − training mean| / sample standard deviation. Flag above 3; constant columns contribute zero.

IQR

Training fences Q1 − 1.5 IQR and Q3 + 1.5 IQR. Flag strictly outside; zero-IQR columns contribute zero.

Great Expectations

Not-null on all columns; inclusive training quantiles 0.1% and 99.9%. Score: fraction of active checks violated. A generic statistical baseline: no domain-specific category, format or cross-field rules.

Evaluated variants

Stable row scores require explicit adaptations

A fitted ETL detector must score a row without consulting other evaluation rows. Contract tests check the 17 row-level detectors; the time-window monitor has a different contract.

ECOD and COPOD

PyOD 3.6.7 recomputes tails and skew with the evaluated batch. Here ranks and skew signs are frozen from training.

Copula motivation versus score

COPOD adds marginal contributions: it is not a fitted general joint copula. ECOD takes the max of both log tails and the skew term.

Neural scoring

VAE decodes the latent mean; DIF reuses a training scaler; AE1SVM padding reweights its last row. These are algorithm changes.

Evidence boundary

Bounded tests support batch independence, not universal equivalence. Separate ablations remain open; historical results keep their actual adapted configurations.

Exploratory correction  not a replacement of archived results

Historical pool preparation needs revalidation

Historical path

Column filtering, category vocabulary and conflicting-label removal happen before the split. Training-only medians and scaling do not isolate those earlier decisions.

Constructed negative controls: changing only the evaluation categories changes the encoded width from 5 to 3; an evaluation duplicate's label can remove a reference row. The effect on AP is not yet quantified.

pool-train-only-v1

Consumes source-bound frozen positions. Fitted schema, imputation and scaling use training rows only. Cross-partition duplicates are rejected; evaluation rows are retained. Targets are excluded from preparation.

Bounded checks enforce full line and branch coverage for the two new modules and detect three leakage defects.

Still outstanding

Historical memberships were recomputed (not originally archived) and replay-validated: the Z-score reproduces all 100 archived per-seed results. Replaying the studies on the train-only path remains outstanding. Old protocols and results are preserved, and no corrected pool-performance claim is available.

Across the three example datasets

Unsupervised detection works in
one of three regimes

Fraud
too extreme

0.17% positives, and the label mixes every defect. Best F1 of seventeen detectors is 0.35 (Deep SVDD), best precision 0.34 (HBOS).

Cardio
the fit

9.6%, rare and distinct. Isolation Forest F1 0.62, AP 0.70. The regime the detectors are built for.

Water
wrong regime

61% majority target. Every ROC-AUC 0.45 to 0.51: the detectors are at chance. A useful negative result.

The question is less "rules or ML" than "does this dataset have rare, outlying anomalies at all". The synthetic dataset sits in the middle at 17.5% and is where the per-type structure is measured. Pipeline cost narrows the shortlist: LOF and One-Class SVM were not run on the full fraud dataset (expected to be too costly, not measured).

What was harder than the plan assumed

Findings from the build

Each of these became a paragraph in the paper. The recurring lesson: the setup around a detector, the operating point, the regime and the pipeline cost matter as much as the algorithm.

1
The operating point is a lever

Contamination equal to prevalence instead of a fixed 10% moves Isolation Forest fraud precision from 0.01 to 0.28 on the seed of the paper's table (0.20 on average over five seeds).

2
One regime out of three

Cardio F1 0.62. Fraud, mixed label: nothing usable. Water Quality: at chance.

3
ML is weak on missing values

Imputing removes the signal a not-null rule catches for free: GX recall 1.00 against 0.00 to 0.28 for the classical learned models.

4
GX 1.x is a full API rewrite

Per-row failures need the COMPLETE result format plus an explicit index column; found by probing the installed library.

5
PyOD scores can depend on the batch

ECOD, COPOD and DIF use the scored rows for their statistics, the VAE draws a random code, AE1SVM crashes on a last batch of one row. Recorded adaptations, with 17 batch-independence checks; not upstream-equivalence claims.

6
Drift is not a per-row problem

Once diluted by clean recent rows, the window mean has moved by about a tenth of a standard deviation.

A mutant is a deliberately broken copy of the code

- flags = scores > info["threshold"]

A tool makes many copies, each with one small plausible bug, and runs the test suite on every copy. The change here is real: a score equal to the training threshold would now raise an alert.

A survivor matters because it is either a behaviour no test protects (a gap to close with a new test), or a change that cannot be observed (an equivalent mutant, next slide). It stays in the count of eligible mutants (the denominator of killed / eligible) until one of the two is shown.

Mutation testing, 3 of 5

Some changes cannot be seen, and only the owner may say so

Equivalent mutant

A change that does not alter what the program does, so no test can fail on it. For an integer count n, changing n > 0 to n >= 1 gives the same answer for every input.

nn > 0n >= 1 (mutant)
-1FalseFalse
0FalseFalse
1TrueTrue
2TrueTrue

Candidates in this work, not approved: a dtype coercion on an array that is already float, zip(strict=) on lists of equal length, flush= on a print.

Why approval is required

Calling a survivor equivalent removes it from the denominator and raises the score. If the author of the tests could do that alone, the author would grade their own work. The repository records each equivalence, with its reason, as accepted by the owner.

Status here

The candidates stay in the denominator. The four equivalences accepted earlier for the LSTM autoencoder were accepted by the repository owner, who is also the sole author and reviewer: owner approval, not independent review.

Takeaway

Rules and machine learning
are complementary by anomaly type.

Across the three examples, unsupervised detection pays off only on Cardio, where anomalies are rare outliers. On 100 labelled pool problems, rankings need train-only revalidation. On 42 real tables with injected types, the synthetic pattern holds only in part, which is why the real-data matrix is the differentiator.

The pipeline, the 152-dataset pool, the protocols and the verification layer are in place. The remaining P0 corrections and the train-only replay are what remains. The photograph is the cover illustration again: one raised pad.
