# Score-distribution shift (T170) and threshold obsolescence (T171) - exploratory, 2026-11-11

Code: `research/score_shift.py` (L4, `research/tests/test_score_shift.py`). Evidence:
`verification/evidence/alert_budgets/score_shift/` (`runs/`, `summary.json`). Reproduce:
`python research/score_shift.py --output <dir>/runs` then `--summarize <dir>/runs --output <dir>/summary.json`.

## Design (T170)

Five fast classical detectors (Isolation Forest, ECOD, HBOS, PCA, COPOD) fitted once per problem and seed
(the 100-problem pool, seeds 42, 1, 2, 3, 4). The deployment threshold is the training-score quantile for
budgets of 1%, 2% and 5% and stays fixed while the evaluated scores change. Units are standard deviations
of the training scores. Scenarios, each a different kind of change:

| scenario | applied to | ranking | what it represents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| location -1, -0.5, +0.5, +1 | held-out scores only | unchanged | absolute score level moves |
| scale 0.5, 2 (about the training median) | held-out scores only | unchanged | score spread changes |
| noise 0.5, 1 | held-out scores only | degraded | scores become less informative |
| monotone transform (arcsinh) | training and held-out scores | unchanged | calibration and evaluation shift together |
| feature shift 0.25, 0.5, 1 | held-out features, rescored by the model | changes | an actual population drift |

Prevalence is untouched here (T169). Labels only score the alerts. Synthetic shifts, one machine.

Denominators: 98 problems, 2,450 fits (5 detectors x 5 seeds x 98); Hepatitis and wine have fewer than
100 training rows, cannot support a 1% budget, and their 50 fits are recorded as errors, not dropped
silently. 0 problems failed to load.

## Findings (pooled medians over problems at the 2% budget; baseline alert rate is 1.10 times the budget)

- A shift or a scale change of the evaluated scores, with an intact ranking, leaves ROC-AUC (0.661) unchanged
  but moves the alert volume a lot: location +0.5 gives 2.4 times the budget and +1 gives 4.6 times; -1 gives
  0.35 times; scale 2 gives 6.0 times and scale 0.5 gives 0.10 times. Recall changes with it (0.057 baseline;
  0.205 at +1; 0.002 at scale 0.5) and precision falls (0.193 to 0.169, 0.040).
- Noise lowers ROC-AUC (0.661 to 0.633 and 0.608) and raises alert volume to 1.5 and 2.5 times the budget.
- A feature drift is much more damaging: alert volume 14, 22 and 32 times the budget for 0.25, 0.5 and 1 sigma,
  ROC-AUC 0.581, 0.580 and 0.566, precision 0.128, 0.113 and 0.101 (recall rises to 0.50-0.71 only because the
  detector flags most of the population).
- A monotone transform of both calibration and evaluation scores keeps the alerts (median ratios identical to
  the baseline), so a shift that affects both is harmless, while the same size of shift affecting the
  evaluation alone is not.
- A nominal alert budget therefore says nothing about the future alert volume unless the score distribution is
  stable relative to calibration.

## Obsolescence (T171): when a fixed threshold should be treated as unreliable

Conventions fixed before the shift results were examined (`VOLUME_BAND`, `KS_LIMIT`, `AUC_DROP`,
`PRECISION_FLOOR`) and not tuned on them. The stratification of the volume signal by expected alerts
(`ALERT_BINS`) was added after the unshifted false-alarm rate of 24% over all fits was seen, as a
response to it; the 30-alert guidance below is a post-hoc finding, not a pre-specified criterion:

1. Volume signal (label-free): over a monitoring window, the alert rate falls outside 0.5 to 2 times the budget.
2. Drift signal (label-free): the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between the training scores and the recent scores
   exceeds 0.25.
3. Quality signal (needs delayed labels): ROC-AUC falls by at least 0.05 against the reference, or precision at
   the threshold falls below half of its reference.

Window size decides whether the volume signal is usable. Without any shift, the volume signal alone fires on
52% of fits whose window expects fewer than 5 alerts (1,025 fits), on 6% with 5 to 30 expected alerts (800) and
on 0% with at least 30 expected alerts (625); the KS signal fires on 2% of all fits. Use the volume signal only
on windows with at least about 30 expected alerts (for example at least 1,500 rows at a 2% budget).

Detection, for windows with at least 30 expected alerts (25 problems, 625 fits per scenario; the other problems
have too few alerts per window): the volume signal fires on 44% to 49% of fits for a location shift of 0.5
sigma and on 86% to 92% for a shift of one sigma; the KS signal fires on 66% and 100%. A scale change fires the
volume signal on 96% to 97% but the KS signal on only 26%. Under a feature drift the volume signal fires on 60%,
74% and 96% of fits and the KS signal on 67%, 85% and 99%. Noise that degrades the ranking, the deterioration
most likely to be missed, is caught by the volume signal in 5% (0.5 sigma) to 49% (1 sigma) of fits and by the
KS signal in 12% to 22%. Among fits whose ROC-AUC fell or whose precision collapsed, the volume signal fired on
41% to 100% and the KS signal on 17% to 100% depending on the scenario (`summary.json`, `monitors`).

Reading, not a guarantee:

- A threshold is unreliable when signal 1 or signal 2 persists over a full window, or signal 3 is met once labels
  arrive. Signals 1 and 2 are cheap and label-free but they do not always see a ranking loss (noise), so
  delayed labels remain the check on quality.
- Responses differ in cost and risk. Re-computing the quantile on recent unlabelled scores restores the alert
  volume and hides a real incident if the shift is caused by the anomalies themselves (a feature drift), so
  it is justified only when the drift is explained and the ranking is still valid. Retraining is for a quality
  signal or a feature drift. No signal replaces judgement about the cause.
- The conventions (0.5-2 band, 0.25 KS, 0.05 AUC, one half precision) are operational, untuned choices; the
  false-alarm cost of each is measured above, and different costs would move them.

## Limits

Classical detectors only; neural detectors would need refits and thread-controlled runs. Synthetic shifts do
not represent real deployment drift. Median results over heterogeneous problems. The strata by expected alerts
use 25 problems for the large-window analysis. The monitor rates describe these shifts, not an operating
system.
